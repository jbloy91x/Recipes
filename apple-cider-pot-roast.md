# Apple Cider Pot Roast with Creamy Roasted Garlic Mashed Potatoes

Source: https://abrightmoment.com/apple-cider-pot-roast-with-creamy-roasted-garlic-mashed-potatoes/#recipe
Servings: 6 to 8

## For the Apple Cider Pot Roast

- 4 to 6 pounds beef chuck roast, sliced into 6 pieces
- Fine sea salt and ground black pepper
- 3 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 1 yellow onion, thinly sliced, or 2 large shallots
- 6 garlic cloves, sliced
- 6 sage leaves, chopped
- 5 cups apple cider — the drink, not vinegar!
- 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 to 1.5 pounds carrots, stems removed and peeled

## For the Creamy Roasted Garlic Mashed Potatoes

- 2 heads of garlic, tops removed to reveal cloves
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- Fine sea salt
- 2.5 pounds russet potatoes or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and chopped
- 1/2 cup heavy cream (warmed to touch), plus more to desired creamy consistency
- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter

## Oven Method

1. Preheat oven to 325°F.
2. Slice the chuck roast into 5–6 cubes for a better sear. Season all sides generously with salt and black pepper.
3. Heat a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until hot. Add 2 tablespoons olive oil to warm for 30 seconds, then half the seasoned chuck roast. Sear untouched for 2 to 3 minutes, until a deeply browned crust forms and it easily lifts. Flip each piece to cook on another side for another 2 to 3 minutes. Remove and repeat with remaining seasoned chuck roast.
4. Add the remaining 1 tablespoon olive oil, then add the sliced yellow onion, garlic cloves, and chopped sage. Sauté for 4 to 5 minutes, until fragrant and slightly browned. Reduce heat to medium if the aromatics start to brown too quickly.
5. Deglaze with 1/2 cup of the apple cider. Stir until the steam slows down, then add the remaining apple cider. Stir in the Dijon mustard and Worcestershire sauce.
6. Add the browned beef back into the Dutch oven so it's fully submerged in the liquids. Add bay leaves and carrots on top.
7. Bring the liquids to a boil, then cover with lid and place in the oven to slow cook for 3 to 4 hours, until beef is super tender.
8. Remove the carrots and beef to a rimmed dish so only the liquid is left in the Dutch oven. Place over medium-high heat to reduce into a simmer sauce, about 15 to 20 minutes, stirring frequently.
9. Serve the shredded beef and carrots over creamy roasted garlic mashed potatoes with a generous drizzle of the simmer sauce.

## Mashed Potatoes

1. Preheat oven to 400°F.
2. Add the garlic heads (top trimmed off to reveal cloves) to a small sheet of foil. Drizzle with the olive oil and season lightly with salt. Wrap tightly to help trap steam, then place on the middle rack of the oven to cook for 30 to 40 minutes, until slightly browned and tender.
3. In a large pot filled 3/4 with water, season very generously with salt before adding the peeled and chopped potatoes. Boil for 20 to 25 minutes, until fork tender and easily breaks apart.
4. Drain the potatoes then place back into the same pot over LOW heat. Use a potato masher or large fork to mash the potatoes.
5. Add the roasted garlic and mash to combine.
6. Once mashed, turn heat OFF. Add the warmed heavy cream and butter. Mash until smooth and creamy. Continue adding more whole milk or heavy cream to desired creamy consistency. Season with salt and crushed black pepper to taste.

Note: the garlic roasts at 400°F while the roast cooks at 325°F, so do the garlic before the roast goes in, or after it comes out while the sauce reduces.

## Slow Cooker Option

Brown the chuck roast and aromatics first for extra flavor, then cook with the apple cider and other ingredients on low until the beef is tender and easy to shred.

## Instant Pot Adaptation

- Beef: 60 minutes on High pressure, then 15–20 minutes natural release (cubes cook faster than a whole roast, which would need 90+ minutes).
- Liquid: cut the cider back to about 2–3 cups instead of 5 — the Instant Pot doesn't evaporate like the oven does.
- Carrots: don't cook them with the beef for the full time or they'll fall apart. Add them after the beef is done and pressure cook another 8 minutes, or roast them separately.
- Sauce: use Sauté for 10–15 minutes after pressure cooking to reduce it down.
- Everything else stays the same: sear in batches first, sauté the onion/garlic/sage, and the mashed potatoes are unchanged.

## Storage

Store the pot roast, gravy, and mashed potatoes in airtight containers in the refrigerator for up to 3–4 days. Reheat gently until warmed through.
