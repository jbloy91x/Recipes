# Protein Breakfast Bars

A drier, grab-and-go version of the microwave oatmeal bowl — same ingredients, same macros, pressed into bars instead.

**Yield:** 4 bars (or 2 large bars)

## Macros

| | Whole batch | Per bar (1 of 4) | Per large bar (1 of 2) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Calories | ~940 | ~235 | ~470 |
| Protein | ~92 g | ~23 g | ~46 g |
| Carbs | ~88 g | ~22 g | ~44 g |
| Fat | ~28 g | ~7 g | ~14 g |
| Fiber | ~17 g | ~4 g | ~8 g |

## Ingredients

- 70 g old fashioned oats
- 20 g chia seeds
- 30 g peanut butter
- 30 g honey
- 80 g beef protein isolate
- 1–2 tbsp water, only if needed to bring it together

## Method

1. **Grind the oats.** Pulse the oats in a blender or food processor into a coarse flour — this is what makes the bars hold together instead of crumbling.
2. **Warm the binders.** Microwave the peanut butter and honey together for 20–30 seconds, until runny. Stir to combine.
3. **Mix.** Add the protein powder, chia, and oat flour to the warm PB/honey and stir until evenly coated. If it's too dry to clump, add water 1 tbsp at a time.
4. **Press.** Line a small loaf pan (or an 8x8 pan for thinner bars) with parchment. Tip the mix in and press it down hard and evenly — really lean into it. The firmer you press, the better they hold.
5. **Set.** Fridge for at least 2 hours, or overnight, then lift out and cut into 4 bars.

### Baked option (drier and firmer)

Instead of chilling, bake at 325°F (165°C) for 12–15 minutes. Let cool completely in the pan before cutting — they firm up as they cool. Closer to a cookie bar.

## Storage

- Fridge, in a sealed container: up to 4–5 days.
- Freezer: up to a month. Thaw overnight in the fridge.

## Notes

- Beef protein isolate doesn't bind as well as whey, so expect dense and a little crumbly rather than chewy. Grinding the oats and pressing firmly are the two things that matter most.
- The chia absorbs moisture overnight, which helps the bars set — they're usually firmer on day two.
- If a batch still falls apart, add 5–10 g more peanut butter next time (~+30–60 cal, +2.5–5 g fat per 5 g) or 5 g more honey (~+15 cal, +4 g carbs).
- The plain version tastes very earthy — a pinch more salt and mashed banana or chocolate helps a lot (2026-11-17 test run).

## Test run — 2026-11-17

- Added 1/3 cup water — too much. The no-bake version would have been mush, so the batch was baked instead.
- Baked 20 minutes uncovered, then 10 minutes covered, then 10 more minutes — about 40 minutes total — and the middle was still soft. A toothpick with moist crumbs means pull it; cooling in the pan sets it a lot. Only keep baking if it's still wet batter.
- Next time: use far less water (1–2 tbsp max, only if needed) and expect a longer bake than the 12–15 minutes above if any extra water goes in.
- Final verdict after cooling: they set into bars. Taste was very earthy — next time add more salt and something for flavor, either mashed banana or chocolate.

## Batch 2 options — from the 2026-11-17 verdict

Same base, plus 1/2 tsp kosher salt in the mix either way. Pick one flavor; macros are per bar (1 of 4).

### A. Mashed banana
- Add 1 medium mashed banana (~118 g) with the warm PB/honey. Skip added water entirely — the banana brings the moisture.
- Batch ~1,045 cal; per bar ~261 cal, ~23 g protein, ~29 g carbs, ~7 g fat, ~5 g fiber.
- Baked version may need a few extra minutes vs. the plain batch; cool completely in the pan before cutting, same as before.

### B. Chocolate chips
- Fold in 30 g dark chocolate chips after mixing. No change to water.
- Batch ~1,100 cal; per bar ~275 cal, ~23 g protein, ~26 g carbs, ~10 g fat, ~5 g fiber.
- Lighter alternative: 15 g unsweetened cocoa powder + 10 g honey instead of the chips — per bar ~251 cal, ~24 g protein, ~26 g carbs, ~8 g fat, ~5 g fiber.
